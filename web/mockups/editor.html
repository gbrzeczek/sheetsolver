About these fields

Name
Your name for this part, shown on the cutting plan and labels (e.g. "Side left").
Length
The part's finished length in millimetres, before edge banding. For a part with grain, the side along the grain.
Width
The part's finished width in millimetres, before edge banding.
Quantity
How many identical parts you need. With "Best use of the panels I have", this is the most that will be cut; fewer may be cut if they don't fit.
Keep grain direction
Tick this if the part must keep its grain running the same way as the panel's grain (veneered or solid-wood panels, brushed or wood-look melamine). The part is then never turned 90°. Untick it for plain MDF, chipboard or any part whose look doesn't depend on direction — turning parts usually saves material.
Value Shown with the goal Best use of the panels I have
Only used with "Best use of the panels I have": how much you want this part, compared with the others. The plan maximises the total value of the parts it cuts. Leave empty to value parts by their area in mm² (the plan then uses as much of the panel as possible). Set every part to 1 to cut as many parts as possible.

About these fields

Name
Your name for this panel, shown on the cutting plan (e.g. "Birch ply 18 mm").
Length
The panel's long side, as delivered, in millimetres. For a panel with grain, the side along the grain. For "Shortest length from one panel", this is the maximum length available.
Width
The panel's short side, as delivered, in millimetres. For "Shortest width from one panel", this is the maximum width available.
Quantity
How many panels of this size you have. Leave empty for "as many as needed".
Cost
Used only to compare panel sizes when several are listed: a lower total cost is preferred. Leave empty to use the panel's area.

Goal

What the plan should optimise. Use as few panels as possible: cut every part, using the fewest panels; panels are used in the order listed. Best use of the panels I have: the panels can't fit every part, so choose which parts to cut to get the most out of them (by part value — see Value). Shortest length from one panel: you have one panel of fixed width and any length you need (e.g. a long board or a roll); cut every part using the shortest possible length. Shortest width from one panel: the same, but the length is fixed and the width is minimised.

Saw and cutting

Saw blade thickness (kerf)
mm

The width of material each cut removes, in millimetres — the thickness of your saw blade's teeth, not the blade body. Every cut between two parts uses this much material, so parts are spaced by it. Typical values: 3.2–4.4 mm for panel and table saws, 2.2–2.8 mm for track saws. Set 0 only if your dimensions already include it.

Cutting stages

How many times a piece is turned and cut again. Stage 1: full-length cuts across the whole panel, splitting it into strips. Stage 2: each strip is cut across into pieces. Stage 3: each piece is cut once more, across stage-2 cuts. More stages let parts nest more tightly, so less waste, but mean more handling at the saw. 2 suits simple strip cutting; 3 suits most panel-saw work; "Unlimited" gives the least waste with the most handling: cuts may then go either way at every stage, and the last-stage trimming setting does not apply.

Last-stage trimming

Whether a part may be cut free from a small offcut after the last stage. Allowed (recommended): a strip may hold parts of different sizes; a final trimming cut separates each part from its offcut. Not allowed: at the last stage every part must fill its strip exactly, with no offcut — fewer cuts, more waste. One part per strip: every part in a last-stage strip is the same part — the simplest plans to cut, the most waste.

First cuts

Which way the first, full-panel cuts run. Either (recommended): the solver tries both and keeps the better plan. With one panel size, every panel in the plan is cut the same way; with several sizes, each panel may be cut differently. The search time is shared between the two directions. Along the length: rip the panel into long strips first, then cut the strips across — for saws or workflows that always rip first. Across the length: cut the panel across into shorter strips first. Parts with grain keep their direction whichever you choose. With unlimited stages, cuts may always go either way.

Search

Time limit
s

How long the solver may search for a better plan. The first plan usually appears within a second; longer searches find plans with less waste. You can stop the search at any time and keep the best plan so far.
